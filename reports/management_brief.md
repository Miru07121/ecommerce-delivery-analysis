# Management brief: delivery promises and customer experience

**Decision:** Where should an operations manager begin investigating missed delivery promises?

**Scope:** 99,441 historical Olist orders, with 96,470 valid delivered orders eligible for delivery metrics. Independent portfolio analysis of public Brazilian data; not a current operational audit.

## Findings

1. **6.77% of eligible orders arrived late (6,534 orders).** Late orders missed their promise by a median of seven calendar days.
2. **Review outcomes are strongly associated with delivery status.** Poor scores occur on 62.42% of reviewed late orders versus 9.27% of reviewed on-time orders. Restricting to records created on/after delivery changes the comparison to 25.09% versus 9.25%. Timing and selection materially affect the size of the association.
3. **Prioritize both rate and volume.** RJ has a 12.11% late rate across 12,350 eligible orders, including 1,495 late orders. SP has 1,820 late orders across 40,494 eligible orders, but a lower 4.49% late rate. High volume alone does not imply worse performance.

## Recommended actions

| Action | Proposed owner | Evidence needed before implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Investigate RJ delivery lanes and SP exception volume | Logistics analyst | Carrier scans, lane, promised-service level and pickup timestamps |
| Review high-volume seller exceptions | Seller operations | Handoff compliance, stock readiness and carrier pickup evidence; order lateness alone does not establish seller fault |
| Pilot proactive delay notifications | Customer experience lead | Contact logs, randomized or matched comparison, complaint rates and response costs |

**Measurement plan:** Track late rate, median/p90 delay and poor-review rate with stable definitions and review-response coverage. Compare similar lanes and purchase periods. For notifications, run a prospective randomized pilot where feasible; predefine the outcome and guardrails. Do not claim savings until intervention costs and measured results are available.

**Limits:** Historical observational data; incomplete outcomes for some cohorts; review selection; no carrier identifier or cost model in this analysis. These findings support investigation, not causal attribution, staff performance judgments or projected revenue gains.
